01

Summary & problem

The parental-control market is growing (~$1.76B in 2026, projected ~$4.12B by 2034) but is dominated by aging, surveillance-first products. Their core model is failing: tech-savvy teens bypass controls, monitoring doesn't reliably change behavior (per Meta's own research), and heavy surveillance erodes the parent–child trust it depends on. Meanwhile only ~11% of features across leading apps support teen self-regulation — the entire category is built for the parent, not the child.

The opportunity

Build a guardian teens don't want to defeat — one that grows from protection toward self-regulation as the child matures, treats the teen as a first-class user, and covers the risks incumbents miss (starting with AI companions).

02

Goals & non-goals

Goals (v1)

  • Deliver trustworthy, low-noise safety alerts parents act on
  • Ship the Protect / Guide / Coach age-mode framework
  • Give teens a genuine self-regulation surface of their own
  • Provide age-appropriate guardrails for AI-companion use
  • Acquire via a useful free tier; convert to one simple family plan
  • Privacy-by-design and transparency as default, not add-ons

Non-goals (v1)

  • Full covert surveillance / stealth monitoring
  • Adult-security bundle (identity theft, data removal)
  • Clinical diagnosis or treatment of mental-health conditions
  • Console & smart-TV coverage (deferred to v3)
  • Enterprise / school-district administration
  • Guaranteeing 100% un-bypassable restriction (impossible; we design for it instead)

Metric definitions

  • Trust proxy — % of teen accounts that keep transparency mode on (vs attempts to disable/hide).
  • Activation — a family completes setup for ≥ 1 child device and receives ≥ 1 weekly summary.

05

Product principles

  • Trust over surveillance. Default to consent and transparency; the teen can see what's shared. "Nothing hidden, from anyone."
  • Grow, don't police. The product's job is to work itself out of a job — moving control to the child over time.
  • Signal over noise. One accurate alert beats ten anxious ones. Precision is the moat.
  • Design for the platform's reality. Work with iOS constraints (on-device, consent-based), don't fight them.
  • Outcomes over screen time. We optimize for wellbeing and healthy habits, not raw minutes blocked.
06

The age-mode framework

Haloo's central differentiator. A child's account occupies one mode; a parent can advance it (with the teen's visibility) as trust and age grow. Mode changes what the product does and who holds the controls.

ModeAge (typical)Control sits withWhat it emphasizes
Protect~6–10ParentStrong filtering, tight time limits, safe defaults, minimal teen UI.
Guide~11–13SharedShared visibility, negotiated limits, first self-set goals, gentle nudges.
Coach~14+Teen-led, parent safety-netTeen sets own limits & goals; parent sees only high-severity safety signals.

Mode transitions are a designed moment — a shared "graduation" that reduces surveillance as a reward for trust, directly countering the circumvention and trust problems (PB-1, PB-3).

08

Detailed requirements

E1 · Core controls

P0

The credible baseline every competitor has; table stakes to be considered at all.

As a parent, I want to set screen-time schedules, per-app limits, and content filtering across my child's devices, so basic safety and balance work without constant policing.

Acceptance criteria

  • Parent can set daily time budgets and downtime windows per child and per app.
  • Web/content filtering with age-tiered defaults (Protect auto-applies the strictest tier).
  • Setup works on iOS and Android; cross-device limits tied to the child's Haloo account.
  • On tamper (uninstall attempt, profile removal), parent is notified and device re-locks per policy.
  • Free tier covers ≥ 1 child device with core limits + filtering.

E2 · Trustworthy alert engine

P0the moat

Precision is the defensible differentiator vs Bark-style alert volume.

As a parent, I want alerts only when something genuinely matters, with enough context to act, so I trust the app instead of muting it.

Acceptance criteria

  • Alerts classify severity into tiers (info / concern / urgent); urgent (e.g. self-harm, predatory contact) never suppressed.
  • Analysis covers text and images; multilingual support for the top launch locales.
  • Each alert includes context (what, where, why flagged) and a suggested next step — never raw dumps.
  • Measured false-positive rate < 15% on the evaluation set before GA.
  • Parent feedback ("useful / not relevant") feeds back into tuning.

E3 · Age-mode framework

P0

The product's identity; converts "one mode for all ages" incumbents into legacy.

As a family, we want the app to shift control toward the child as they grow, so supervision eases as a reward for trust rather than a fixed regime.

  • Each child account is in exactly one mode: Protect, Guide, or Coach.
  • Mode sets defaults for control ownership, alert sharing, and teen-UI visibility (per §06 table).
  • Advancing a mode is a shared, visible action; the teen is notified what changes about their privacy.
  • In Coach mode, the parent receives only urgent-tier safety alerts by default.

E4 · Teen self-regulation

P1whitespace

Closes the 89/11 gap; the reason a teen keeps the app instead of bypassing it.

As a teen, I want my own view where I set my limits, see my patterns, and get help privately, so the app respects me as its user too.

  • Teen has a dedicated dashboard: their screen-time insights, self-set app limits, and streaks/goals.
  • Optional lightweight mood check-ins (non-clinical) visible to the teen; sharing with parent is teen-controlled in Coach mode.
  • Always-available, private path to vetted help resources (e.g. crisis lines) — reachable without parent visibility.
  • Self-set limits are honored the same as parent-set ones; teen sees exactly what parent can and can't view.

E5 · AI-companion guardrails

P1first-mover

2026's fastest-moving risk and incumbents' clearest blind spot.

As a parent, I want to know if my child is forming intense relationships with AI chatbots and set age-appropriate limits, because this is invisible to today's tools.

  • Detect use of AI-companion apps/surfaces; surface usage patterns (frequency, time-of-day, escalation).
  • Age-gate or limit AI-companion access per mode; urgent-tier alert on high-risk content (self-harm, grooming-like patterns).
  • Guardrails degrade gracefully where platform APIs limit visibility; be transparent about coverage gaps.

E6 · Wellbeing insights

P2

Anchors the brand on outcomes, not minutes blocked; supports premium tier.

As a family, we want a weekly "balance summary" and prompts for good conversations, so the app helps us talk rather than surveil.

  • Weekly balance summary per child (trends, not raw logs), framed around wellbeing.
  • Conversation prompts tied to what changed that week; never diagnostic language.
  • Insights are shareable within the family, never sold or used for ads.
09

Privacy, security & compliance

Non-negotiable — one incident is fatal in this category. Treated as product requirements, not legal boilerplate.

  • Privacy-by-design. Prefer on-device analysis; minimize what leaves the device; no selling or ad-targeting of family data.
  • Transparency mode. The teen can always see what is monitored and shared; hidden/stealth monitoring is out of scope.
  • Regulatory fit. COPPA (under-13), and the 2024–2026 child-safety wave (age verification, minors' privacy, design mandates); GDPR-K where applicable.
  • Security. Encryption in transit & at rest; third-party audit before GA; strict access controls on sensitive signals.
  • Safety escalation. Clear, documented handling for urgent-tier signals; non-diagnostic framing; real professional resources, not in-house clinical claims.

Risks & open questions

Key risks

  • Platform dependency. Apple/Google tighten APIs or ship free equivalents → own the collaborative/wellbeing layer they won't.
  • Trust incident. A leak is existential → privacy-by-design + external audit pre-GA.
  • Efficacy scrutiny. "Control" branding ages badly → anchor on wellbeing outcomes.
  • Clinical liability. Mood features invite exposure → non-diagnostic, professional-backed.

Open questions

  • Final product name (Haloo working name; alternatives under review).
  • Exact false-positive threshold & eval dataset for GA.
  • How much on-device vs cloud analysis, given iOS limits & cost?
  • Which launch locales for multilingual alerts?
  • Mode age bands — fixed defaults or fully parent-configurable?
